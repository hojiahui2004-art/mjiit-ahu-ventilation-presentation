Operating Principles: CAV vs. VAV

Fluid Dynamics & Flow Modulation

CAV System: Fixed Airflow (100% CFM)

Variable Supply Temperature
• Constant Fan Energy (No VFD speed modulation)
• Overcools & Reheats at partial thermal load

VAV System: Modulating Airflow (VFD)

Constant Supply Temperature (13°C)
• VFD Fan Power: 34.3% (Dynamically scales with thermal demand)
• VAV Damper Modulates Flow to prevent overcooling
70% Thermal Load (VAV Fan: 34.3% Power)
Slide 03

Evolution of Ventilation Technology

60-Year Engineering Progression
1960s–70s: Fixed-Speed CAV Baseline Waste

Single-speed supply blowers with constant CFM volume. High overcooling, severe humidity issues, and no capacity modulation.

1980s–90s: VFD-Driven VAV -30% Fan Power

Variable Frequency Drives (VFD) and terminal VAV boxes introduced dynamic CFM scaling according to thermal zone demand.

2000s–10s: DOAS & Heat Pipes Decoupled Latent

Dedicated Outdoor Air Systems (DOAS) with wrap-around heat pipes decoupled sensible and latent loads, eliminating active reheat.

2020s+: IoT CO2 Sensor DCV & AI 38.9% Energy Saved

Demand-Controlled Ventilation (DCV) guided by real-time IoT CO2 sensors, delivering precision IAQ and full MS 1525 / DOSH compliance.

Slide 06

Tropical Overcooling & Reheat Challenge

Psychrometric Energy Trap
1. The Deep Cooling Dilemma

Standard AHUs must chill air to 12.0°C just to wring out ambient water vapor from 21.0 g/kg down to 8.8 g/kg.

2. Zone Overcooling Penalty

Direct 12°C supply drops room temperature to 23.8°C, breaching thermal comfort and causing sick building complaints.

3. Energy Reheat Waste

Activating electric heaters to warm overcooled air wastes up to 37% of total HVAC power—a double penalty!
